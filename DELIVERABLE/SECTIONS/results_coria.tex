\section{Results and Discussion}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{mdHODMDit Applied to the CORIA Dataset}
\label{sec:results_coria}

This section presents the application of the multidimensional iterative Higher-Order
Dynamic Mode Decomposition (mdHODMDit) algorithm to the three NH$_3$/H$_2$ DNS datasets
from CORIA described in Section~\ref{sec:datasets}.

% -------------------------------------------------------
\subsubsection{Data Structure and Preprocessing}
\label{sec:coria_preprocessing}
% -------------------------------------------------------

The DNS output for each temperature case is stored as a four-dimensional tensor
$\mathcal{T} \in \mathbb{R}^{N_{\mathrm{var}} \times N_x \times N_y \times N_t}$, where
the axes correspond to the reactive scalar variables, the streamwise spatial coordinate
$x$, the cross-stream coordinate $y$, and time $t$, respectively.
Each snapshot contains $N_{\mathrm{var}} = 20$ fields (19 species and temperature);
the full spatial domain consists of $2400 \times 200$ cells at 50~$\mu$m resolution.

Before analysis, the tensor is restricted to the region of physical interest.
In the streamwise direction, indices $x_{60:1000}$ are retained, corresponding to the
range $[0.3, 5.0]$~cm downstream of the splitter plate, which encompasses the near-field
flame stabilisation region and the initial development of the shear layer.
In time, the first 74 snapshots are discarded to remove the initial transient, so the
analysis is performed on the statistically developed portion of the signal.
The temporal resolution is $\Delta t = 40~\mu$s.
Prior to decomposition, each scalar field is auto-scaled to zero mean and unit variance
in order to make the modal amplitudes comparable across species with very different
concentration ranges.

% -------------------------------------------------------
\subsubsection{Parameter Calibration}
\label{sec:coria_params}
% -------------------------------------------------------

The analysis is performed over a grid of Hankel delay parameters
\begin{equation}
    d \in \{10,\, 15,\, 20,\, 25,\, 30,\, 35,\, 40,\, 45\}
    \label{eq:d_values}
\end{equation}
and tolerance values
\begin{equation}
    \varepsilon_{\mathrm{tol}} \in
    \{5\times10^{-3},\, 2\times10^{-2},\, 3\times10^{-2},\, 4\times10^{-2},\,
      5\times10^{-2},\, 7\times10^{-2},\, 1\times10^{-1},\, 2\times10^{-1}\}.
    \label{eq:tol_values}
\end{equation}
The same tolerance $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{tol}}$ governs both the HOSVD truncation
(equation~\eqref{eq:hosvd_trunc}) and the amplitude threshold applied to retain DMD
modes, so that a single parameter controls the compression level throughout the
algorithm.

% -------------------------------------------------------
\subsubsection{Identification of Physical Frequencies}
\label{sec:coria_frequencies}
% -------------------------------------------------------

A DMD mode frequency is considered \emph{physical} if the corresponding spectral peak
appears consistently across multiple values of the delay parameter $d$ and is not
sensitive to the choice of $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{tol}}$.
Formally, a Strouhal number $St_k$ is flagged as physical if it is identified as a
dominant peak (i.e.\ its normalised amplitude exceeds a fixed threshold) in at least
half of the parameter combinations $\{(d_i, \varepsilon_j)\}$ included in the sweep.
This robustness criterion filters out artefacts of the decomposition that arise for
particular choices of the delay window or compression level, retaining only coherent
structures whose temporal periodicity is an intrinsic feature of the flow.

The Strouhal number is defined throughout as
\begin{equation}
    St = f \cdot \frac{\ell}{U_b},
    \label{eq:strouhal}
\end{equation}
where $\ell = 3$~mm is a reference length scale of the mixing layer and $U_b$ is the
bulk convective velocity, taken as $U_b = 21.34$, $24.29$, and $27.14$~m~s$^{-1}$ for
the 1200~K, 1500~K, and 1700~K cases, respectively.

\subsubsection{Spatial DMD Modes (1500~K Reference Case)}

of the retained reactive scalar fields.
Figures~\ref{fig:mode_021_coria}--\ref{fig:mode_042_coria} show the real part of the

% -------------------------------------------------------
\subsubsection{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion_coria}
% -------------------------------------------------------

The mdHODMDit algorithm, in its multidimensional HOSVD-based formulation, successfully
extracts physically coherent spatio-temporal modes from the high-dimensional DNS tensors
of all three temperature cases without requiring any reshaping of the data into matrix
form.
The independent truncation of each tensor mode by HOSVD enables a more targeted noise
filtering compared to a standard SVD applied to a vectorised snapshot matrix: the
variable axis is compressed according to the co-variance structure among the 20 reactive
scalars, while the spatial axes are compressed independently along $x$ and $y$, allowing
the method to exploit the anisotropy of the flow geometry.

The dominant Strouhal numbers identified in the 1500~K reference case lie in the range
$St \approx 0.21$--$0.42$.

\paragraph{Physical interpretation of the dominant modes}

The spatial modes show a clear progression from large-scale shear-layer structures at
low $St$ to finer-scale alternating patterns at higher $St$, reflecting the multi-scale
nature of the reactive flow dynamics.
The modes at $St \approx 0.21$--$0.25$ are consistent with the primary convective
instability of the non-premixed mixing layer, in which vortices form and grow
downstream of the splitter plate.
The higher-frequency modes at $St \approx 0.37$--$0.42$ likely correspond to secondary
instabilities or harmonics of the primary roll-up, and their high amplitude in the
1500~K case indicates a vigorous nonlinear energy transfer between the two frequency
bands.
The fact that the spatial support of all identified modes is concentrated in the region
$x/h \lesssim [PLACEHOLDER]$ suggests that the dominant dynamics are governed by the
near-field development of the shear layer rather than by the far-field flame structure.
